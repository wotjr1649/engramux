## 2. What M14 settled, and the two sentences that have to travel with it

**The interval holds a better rung and it does not hold a clean one.** At 7,500 ppm — a value M13's
spacing could not see — four of the five classes are at or above their weight-0 baseline. `a reply's
own words` reaches **93 of 139**, one above what 5,000 and 10,000 both reached; `a command line`
reaches 346 of 534; and `a touched path` reaches **57 of 120**, which is **the first document any of
backlog 53's three candidates has lifted on the class that vetoed the other two**. The fifth class is
the veto and it is 74 of 96 against a baseline of 79.

**The veto is one class and it never comes back.** `an error message` is short of 79 at every rung in
the ladder, best at 78 where the reply class has already fallen to 86 and a touched path to 55 — so
the rungs where the harm is smallest are the rungs where the gain is going. The measured reason was
in the spec before the ladder was chosen: that class's own targets have a median coverage of **3,100
ppm** against the gain classes' 13,953 and 23,026, because a failure line is cut out of tool output
and tool output is what the long documents are. Every rung high enough to lift a reply is far above
an error message's own share of its document.

**Why this is a finding and not a grid chosen from an answer already seen, and the honest half goes
first.** **The interval came from M13's table, not from M13's pre-registration.** `2ab9de3`
registered M13's ladder and condition and names no range; the sentence naming 5,000 to 20,000 was
written in `c6321f9`, after the answer was visible. **Choosing where to look from a result is
ordinary. Choosing the rule after seeing which answer it gives is not** — and M14's condition is
M11's own sentence over **every candidate of every class**, 21, 5 and 4 times as many documents in
the three harm classes, which is **strictly harder to pass than the one it follows and never
easier**. That is the whole defence and it does not need the other one. Nothing of M13's moved: its
ladder, its condition, its licensed list and every pinned count are where they were.

**Three anchors, and only one of them tests the rule.** Weight 0 reproduced M11's 6, 76, 17, 13, 19
on the sampled 25 and M12's 336, 56, 79 over every candidate — and neither says anything about
coverage, because at weight 0 no id set is passed at all. The one that does is the third:
**sixty of M13's own counts over these same populations at the five shared rungs, recall and
`demoted` alike, all reproduced.** Without it a first run would have been pinning whatever came out
of it. It is also what licensed the retirement of M13's supplementary arm, which is where M14's cost
was paid: **M13 goes from 63.2 s to 16.3 s and the pair costs 95.4 s.**

**What the break-it pass says that no column of the table does.** Relaxing the condition from
*improves one and regresses none* to *improves one or regresses none* licenses **all nine rungs**.
Every rung regresses a harm class, so that disjunction's second half is false everywhere — which
means **every rung in the ladder improves a gain class**, and the verdict is decided entirely by the
harm arm at every threshold. There is no rung whose gain is the thing that fails.

**What that leaves of backlog 53.** The row keeps its place and loses the sweep it was waiting on.
All three named candidates are measured, three tests own them, and nothing ships — `Search` still
passes 0 and nil. What is untried is **the weight**, which M12, M13 and M14 all fixed at 5 and only
M11 ever swept, and the reserved-slot candidate that demotes nothing and so has no harm arm of M11's
shape at all.

## 5. Things that will bite

1. **A wall-clock figure from one run is not comparable to one from another run on this machine.**
   Session 25 recorded `TestGateM13…` at 58 s; the same unchanged test measured **63.18 s** here a
   few hours later, and its own brief quoted it at 50 s. **Read a ratio inside one run** — here M14's
   9,060 searches took 79.1 s against M13's 5,886 at 63.2 s, so 1.54× the searches cost 1.34× the
   wall and the one-scan set builder is the difference. Treat any absolute second figure in this
   repository as a range.
2. **`scripts/race.sh`'s 90 minutes is a per-test-binary timeout and not a budget for the run**, and
   this is now the tightest thing in the repository. The script says so in its own comment; a
   hand-off that reads it as a run budget prices the next gate against the wrong number, and
   session 25's *whole run took 43 minutes* is a true sentence about a different quantity. The
   binary that matters is `internal/search`, and it measured **3,473.0 s — 57.9 minutes of 90**
   with M14 in it, against 2,500.2 s the day before. **Do not read all of that as the gate.** The
   multiplier is stable across the two days — 16.5× then 15.9× — so the 973 s jump is about sixteen
   times a 66 s jump in the ordinary run, and only 32 s of that 66 is the two gates' net change:
   **about half is M14 and half is the day**, the three earlier readings spreading 2,110.8 s to
   2,563 s. A third of the guard is left, one more gate of M14's size lands at about **79 minutes**
   with 11 minutes of margin against a day's spread of 7.5, and the script's comment says the
   session that adds it raises the timeout in the same commit and says what it re-measured. **The
   owner's answer on 2026-09-07 was to leave 90m and record the measurement**, because a hang guard
   raised without need makes a real hang take longer to surface.
3. **A `sed` pattern that escapes an ampersand in its left-hand side matches nothing and says
   nothing.** `&` is only special in the *replacement*; `\&` in the pattern does not match a literal
   `&`, so a mutation written as `if improved \&\& !regressed` was never applied. It cost nothing
   because the script asserts the mutation is present before running the suite and prints
   **MUTATION NEVER APPLIED** when it is not — which is AGENTS.md's own row working. A pass that
   greps for `--- FAIL` without that check would have reported `ok` for a suite that ran unmutated.
4. **A mutation that turns `a && b` into `a` does not compile**, because `b`'s only reference goes
   with it. The mutation that works is `a || b`, which changes the answer instead of removing the
   reference — it is what produced §2's last finding.
5. **A `bash` heredoc containing a backtick is refused before it runs**, by a guard that reads it as
   an unclosed command substitution however the body is quoted. Prose about this codebase is full of
   backticks, so use the file-edit tool for that text.
6. **`.capture/`-dependent gates skip silently without it.** M11, M12, M13, M14 and M8's corpus arm
   all do. A green run on a machine with no corpus has measured nothing, and the skip is the only
   thing that says so.
7. **Do not paste a `TestPhase4Gate` corpus-mode run anywhere.** One line of it carries a real path.
8. `go test` without `-p 1` is refused by a guard, on the command line and not in `GOFLAGS`.
